## Question, decision, and scope

What minimum durable identity, provenance, applicability, and historical-attribution contract must Governance `Evidence` and `EvidenceUse` expose so that evidence can be reused and interpreted across exact semantic revisions without conflating raw source facts, producer-owned analytical results, or later Governance interpretation?

The decision is whether that semantic boundary can be reconciled sufficiently to admit a first headless Governance evidence capability. This report answers the semantic question; it does not perform the required independent review, architecture reconciliation, or planning admission.

In scope are identity and reuse, correction, source versus use provenance, temporal and subject applicability, analytical compatibility, and the ability to explain a historical evaluation. Out of scope are storage schemas, ingestion, authentication/signature infrastructure, retention implementation, production adapters, analytics ownership redesign, actor identity, and a general verification framework. A semantic obligation to preserve or explicitly bound history does not select a persistence mechanism.

## Executive conclusion

**Recommendation:** reconcile an immutable source/result-reference plus contextual-use contract. An evidence reference identifies a particular attributable recorded assertion, observation, artifact revision, or analytical result, not an eternally true proposition. An evidence use identifies how that exact material was considered for one particular evaluation and claim. The evaluation preserves its actual evidence basis, definitions, temporal frame, applicability decisions, and outcome. Those roles must be distinguishable even when represented together.

**Confidence:** high that the source/use distinction and non-rebinding obligations are necessary for the brief's cases; moderate that this is the smallest sufficient first-capability contract. There is no implementation or interoperability experiment establishing a preferred record layout, identifier scheme, or producer API.

Source-record identity and artifact-reference identity converge when both preserve the exact attributable source revision and support explicit uses. A context-bound representation can also work if it retains that same independent source identity; it then differs in packaging, not in the required semantics. Pure evaluation-only identity and bare artifact-digest identity lose necessary distinctions. Deferring all shared semantics remains safe, but does not answer the stated cross-revision reuse and attribution need. The evidence supports common obligations, not a new platform-wide evidence ontology or storage service.

Three qualifications materially change the current proposal:

1. Evidence must not acquire a *fabricated evaluation binding*. It may and sometimes must retain producer-intrinsic model/revision provenance. The proposed blanket statement that evidence carries no fingerprint is too broad for analytical results.
2. Evidence applicability, requirement applicability, and conformance outcome are separate. An unusable item does not make an applicable requirement `NOT_APPLICABLE`, nor does accepted provenance prove the claim true.
3. IDs and version labels alone are insufficient for historical explanation unless they resolve to the exact definitions and material actually used. The current requirement/assertion value contracts do not supply a durable registry that enforces that resolution.

Implementation remains blocked. Independent adversarial research review, focused ADR/architecture reconciliation, and the existing planning promotion conditions must precede it.

## Candidate models

| Candidate | Meaning under test |
| --- | --- |
| Source record plus uses | A source-qualified immutable record identifies one attributable observation/assertion/result. Uses carry claim/evaluation context. A Governance wrapper is possible, but ownership of the producer's fact stays with the producer. |
| Context-bound record | Evidence is primarily keyed to evaluation/revision. In the pure form, repeated contextual copies do not retain a stable independent source-revision reference. In the linked form, they do. Both forms are evaluated, because linked duplication is not inherently wrong. |
| Artifact/fact plus interpretation | Uses reference an immutable artifact or fact. A bare content digest/value is the weak form; an exact producer/source revision with attributable provenance is the strong form. “The same fact” cannot silently mean “the same numeric value” or “the same true proposition.” |
| Defer shared contract | Keep current Governance and producer facts separate, without a new common evidence obligation. Applicable unproved assertions remain UNKNOWN and implementation remains blocked. This is a valid abstention, not an unsafe design. |

An apparent omitted candidate is **versioned producer handles with no separately allocated Governance evidence ID**. It is viable: an exact source namespace/record/revision reference can supply the source-record role, provided its binding is immutable and sufficiently resolvable. It is the strong artifact/reference candidate, not a fifth ontology. No case requires an additional global ID once that reference is adequate. Conversely, a mutable URL or producer UUID that persists across revisions needs an immutable revision/snapshot qualifier.

The comparison tests semantic roles, not object/table counts. If a context-bound record preserves independent source identity and all use context, it implements the same minimum obligations as source-plus-use despite serializing them together.

### Reuse, rollback, equality, and import

Take a source observation `plant-a/sensor-7/sample-19@original` and two evaluations. One concerns controlled revision `earlier` and the other `rollback`; both revisions have the same model fingerprint, but occur at different times. Alternatively the second evaluation concerns a different fingerprint. A shared source reference can remain unchanged while each use names its exact target and independently judges scope/time suitability. Fingerprint equality never copies approval or evidence applicability from `earlier` to `rollback`. ADR-0008 and its repeated-fingerprint tests establish the history distinction.

Two sensors reporting `20 °C` at the same time are two attributable observations. Two runs producing byte-identical results are two productions when the producer's contract distinguishes runs. Conversely, redelivery of the same exact source record does not create an independent corroborating observation merely because another importer or evaluation saw it. Imported occurrences may have their own capture identities, but their common source lineage must remain visible. No global deduplication algorithm is required. Bare content equality fails this case; qualified source identity plus use succeeds.

To establish this in implementation later, fixtures must show two uses retaining the same exact source reference, independent scope/revision attribution, and duplicate delivery not inflating independent support. They must also reject a claimed source-reference rebind. These are proposed acceptance assets, not new tests in this run.

### Correction, reinterpretation, and temporal meaning

Suppose an observation describes the interval 09:00–10:00, arrives at 10:10, and is used at 10:20. A source correction arrives at 11:00 for the same observed interval. Preserve the original, the correction's exact identity, what it corrects, its source, and when it became available. The 10:20 evaluation remains a record of the original basis. A later reassessment may reach a different result; it must identify its new basis and relationship to the old evaluation.

If only Governance's freshness interpretation changes, keep the source record unchanged and create a new use/evaluation. If a producer changes a calculation or extracted claim, create a new attributable result with input/derivation provenance. A mutable source item with version history is acceptable only when a use pins the version. No immutable event-store implementation is implied.

The time domain matters: simulation ticks are not UTC, receipt time is not observed time, and an absent effective period is not permission to treat evidence as timeless. Endpoint inclusion, uncertainty, and units must be defined by the relevant source/claim contract. A claim about an interval requires the appropriate coverage; one point measurement cannot prove the whole interval without an explicit domain rule. The above times are synthetic proving fixtures, not a universal interval convention.

### Unusable evidence and conformance

Consider an applicable assertion requiring evidence about one resource during an identified period. If no qualifying observation exists, retain a missing-evidence explanation and `UNKNOWN` for that unobserved claim. If a supplied observation is too old, outside the period, mapped to another subject, or produced under unestablished compatible semantics, retain why it was excluded or unresolved. Do not manufacture an Evidence item to represent absence; a gap can belong to the evaluation basis. A known rejected item can have a considered-but-not-relied-on use.

These exclusions alone do not prove the underlying claim false and do not make the requirement out of scope. A sufficient alternative evidence set can still support `PASS`; a single stale item must not poison otherwise sufficient evidence. A *different* assertion requiring a current inspection record can fail when reliable evidence establishes that the required record does not exist. A failed search of an incomplete feed is not that evidence. The assertion owns this distinction.

Malformed or falsely bound input may be rejected at the boundary rather than converted to a business result. This preserves the current evaluator's explicit revision/fingerprint errors. A future use contract must explain unavailable or unusable input without treating every infrastructure error as a real-world failure. No new universal result enum is justified.

### External source, correspondence, trust, and analytical ownership

An external observation may exist before any Arcogine correspondence. Retain its source namespace, external subject, fact/unit, source time, receipt provenance, and known quality/authenticity information. A later use needs an attributable correspondence decision for the intended subject/time. Endpoint naming or coincident IDs do not establish that mapping. Governance can consume a fixture-backed explicit correspondence assertion for a bounded headless case; doing so does not implement Operational ingestion or prove the correspondence authority trusted itself. Unknown mapping remains unknown.

Authenticity of a message, fitness of its source, adequacy of its measurement, and relevance to a claim differ. A later trust revocation or mapping correction produces a new assessment affecting new reliance, while preserving what a historical evaluation used and knew. An immutable statement that was recorded is not a guarantee its content was true. This is required by the proving case and consistent with the repository's provenance distinctions; the full trust mechanism remains deferred.

For analytical evidence, preserve the producing capability, exact result identity, subject model fingerprint, Engine semantics when Engine behavior is involved, explicit result-affecting inputs, run/cursor or result-boundary provenance, and the analytical definition/version needed to interpret the result. A run ID alone does not identify a time-varying result; model plus Engine version alone omits workload, random inputs, ordered commands, and other material inputs from ADR-0015. A consumer-side calculation may add its own analytical definition without taking ownership of Engine rules. Record the resulting provenance chain rather than relabeling it raw observation or structural state.

The original source model is not automatically the use target. A result for design `baseline` may serve as a comparator for candidate `changed`; it is not direct proof of `changed`'s performance. The use must preserve that role and the domain-owned comparison semantics. Cross-version compatibility is a determination about a particular claim/metric and assumptions, not a global equality of Engine versions. Same-version equality also does not establish equal workload, units, scope, or experimental conditions. Without adequate compatibility evidence, comparison stays unsupported; identity does not authorize guessing.

At this baseline, real Engine provenance propagation is incomplete. A fixture can test carrying an explicitly attributed analytical result, but cannot demonstrate a production adapter obtaining missing provenance. There is no license to infer the semantics version from a build name or silently stamp older results with the current version.

### Historical closure, retired execution, and conflict

A historical evaluation must remain explainable after the requirement, assertion, evidence inventory, target model, and applicability policy evolve. Preserve the actual exact evidence/use set and any material exclusions/gaps; resolve the exact requirement/assertion meaning and external governing source; identify the subject fingerprint and optional authoritative revision; and preserve the applicability/evaluation interpretation used. A “latest requirement” lookup or current query for evidence silently changes the historical question. A list of IDs that can be rebound is insufficient.

This does not require retaining every potential item an evaluator never encountered. It requires the selection/coverage basis needed to explain the result, including known material contradictory evidence and exclusion reasons. A claim based on absence needs evidence of search scope/completeness. Two conflicting measurements are not settled by picking the newer arrival or the lower ID unless the claim's recorded policy gives that rule authority. Without a justified resolution, preserve the conflict and do not claim PASS.

Engine retirement does not erase attribution or automatically invalidate a historical result, per ADR-0015. Whether the result remains acceptable for another claim is a new use determination. Similarly, a retained digest with missing content proves neither readability nor meaning. If necessary history is missing or corrupt, report the gap; never substitute a current source revision or re-execute under current semantics. Retention can be bounded, but the remaining explanation and limits must be truthful. No perpetual byte retention or replay guarantee is introduced.

## Minimum surviving semantic contract

The following are **recommendations/inferences**, not existing APIs or accepted architecture. “Preserve” means carry the information or retain an exact authoritative relationship sufficient to recover it; it does not require a field on every record.

### Evidence reference and referent

1. Identify a particular attributable **recorded assertion/observation/result revision** with stable source-qualified reference semantics. It is the record of what a source asserted or produced, not the external subject, numerical value, artifact filename, evaluation, or truth of the proposition.
2. The same complete reference must not resolve to different identity-bearing content or intrinsic provenance. Revised payload, corrected intrinsic provenance, or changed producer interpretation creates a distinct revision/result relationship. Later annotations or trust judgments may reference the original without rebinding it.
3. Identity equality means the same referenced record revision; it does not mean byte equality, logical equivalence, common subject, independent corroboration, current trust, or applicability. A producer's stable logical ID can be reused across its revisions only if the evidence reference also distinguishes those revisions. A bare digest identifies content, not necessarily the attributable source occurrence.
4. Preserve enough source meaning to interpret the record: source/producer, described subject, fact/result and relevant units, source/production time or explicit uncertainty, and material intrinsic provenance. Different origins must remain distinguishable without requiring a closed universal origin enum. Multi-input derivations retain their inputs and producing interpretation.
5. Evidence captured outside Arcogine need not have Arcogine model/revision/continuation identity. Evidence *produced from* an Arcogine model retains that intrinsic provenance. Source authenticity claims, recording provenance, producer semantics, actor participation when actually known, and evaluation authorization must not collapse into one ambiguous field.

No case requires choosing UUID, hash, URI grammar, a global evidence registry, or duplicate producer storage. A producer handle can fulfill these obligations. If a source offers only a mutable reference, an exact attributable capture or source revision is necessary before durable historical reliance; the capture proves what was obtained, not automatically the truth of the source's claims. Unknown intrinsic facts must be explicit and can prevent a use without preventing faithful recording.

### Contextual use and evaluation basis

1. Bind the exact referenced material to one identifiable evaluation occurrence and claim/role. Preserve target model fingerprint, optional verified controlled revision, semantic subject/scope, exact requirement/assertion versions, and temporal frame where material. Do not synthesize an accepted revision for a candidate. More than one use of the same item, even within an evaluation for different roles, must be representable.
2. Preserve a reasoned, attributable applicability/reliance determination: source and subject correspondence, period/freshness, provenance/trust adequacy, analytical meaning/units, and compatibility where required. The determination and its governing interpretation must be recoverable. “Applicable,” “inapplicable,” and “not established” are semantic distinctions here, not a selected API enum.
3. Distinguish applicability from assertion sufficiency and outcome. Only an applicable assertion proven by an adequate basis can pass. Unproved facts remain UNKNOWN; genuine out-of-scope requirements can be NOT_APPLICABLE; adequately established violation can fail. Do not silently default unknown provenance, time, completeness, or compatibility to success.
4. An evaluation's actual basis must be fixed: relied-on uses, relevant considered/excluded material and gaps, definition/interpretation versions, scope and relevant times, and resulting explanation. New knowledge or policy produces a new evaluation/assessment, not mutation of the old basis. Equal outcomes from two evaluations do not make them the same historical occurrence.
5. Exact definition resolution is a prerequisite to historical meaning. Requirement/assertion labels must bind to the definitions actually used, by immutable authoritative resolution or retained definition material. Material changes must not reuse an old semantic definition binding. This is additive to the present identity/equality contracts; it does not change Java equality or assert a durable catalogue already exists.
