This is the ownership question in the split simulation-analytics research series. The other questions — [evidence/provenance](simulation-analytics-evidence-provenance.md), [adapter compatibility](simulation-analytics-adapter-compatibility.md) and [cross-revision comparability](simulation-analytics-cross-revision-comparability.md) — retain their independent, concrete activation conditions. ## Concluded question and verdict

> Which currently supported facts and derived results belong to the Engine/Factory runtime, and when, if ever, does a reusable consumer-neutral owner of named analytical methods become justified?

**Staged, kind-specific admission; no shared analytics owner now.** Engine/Factory own execution truth, supported state/change projections, result-affecting ranking, and exact arithmetic for supported Engine-declared results. A named method that makes population, period, denominator, availability, grouping, attribution or diagnostic judgments not fixed by execution is defined by the capability choosing them, **consumer-local by default**. A shared definition needs a demonstrated common meaning: two actual consumers, one consumer plus an evidenced core verification/Governance need, an admitted outward contract, or divergent duplicates requiring one agreed definition. These are alternative paths; two shipped consumers are not a minimum, but two call sites, a shared evidence holder, fixture-only checks and illustrative objectives do not themselves prove common meaning. The [architecture](../../architecture/overview.md#domain-authority) owns the precise rule.

A new Engine-produced measurement needs a separate reason tied to execution/result-affecting behavior, Engine-wide contractual semantics, or a justified execution-fixed run-to-date aggregate for a consumer whose holder could not capture from establishment. The last option competes with bounded retained/shared delivery and may replace a retention need, **not** interval finality. Existing Engine results are not reclassified just because their computation is reusable or report-only. Blanket “all analytics outside Engine” and the inverse “capture must follow the definition owner” fail as universal policies. Neither a shared analytics module nor a new producer accumulator is admitted.

## Phase 1 — current supported boundary

The Phase 1 descriptive classification remains evidence for the accepted outcome, updated for landed corrections:

| Kind | Current semantic owner and consequence |
|---|---|
| Authoritative state and ordered change (**S**) | Engine/Factory supplies current resource, job, order and pending-work projections; coherent run/model/time/sequence metadata; and post-transition supported events. Authored model meaning/fingerprint remains Factory-owned. |
| Result-affecting dispatch interpretation (**D**) | `combinedQueueDepth` belongs to Engine: it ranks multi-eligible resources and is not physical queue depth for presentation. |
| Fresh-observation-reconstructible supported results (**R-obs**) | `backlog` counts incomplete accepted orders; `completedOrders` counts completed orders, not processing steps; `averageLeadTime` can be reconstructed from observed orders' creation and completion times using the Engine's nonnegative, saturating lead-time sum and empty-set zero; `throughputPerTick` can be reconstructed from completed-order count and **last supported observed time**, giving zero at zero ticks, not a caller-selected-period rate. All four retain their exact Engine-declared meaning and arithmetic. |
| History-dependent supported result (**R-hist**) | `completedSalesValue` accumulates completed-order value in deterministic completion order. Other facts in one fresh supported observation do not generally carry the order prices and history needed to independently reconstruct it; its defining arithmetic remains Engine-owned ([Engine semantics §§10.1–10.2](../../architecture/engine-semantics.md#102-derived-result-accumulator-register)). |
| Public aggregate accessors | Method-level support for `FactoryRuntime.throughput(long)` and sibling accessors remains unclassified by this investigation. The named observation results above are not silently a support ruling on every accessor. |
| Removed completion-credit counter (**former R-hist**) | `busyTicks` and the Engine utilization/occupancy/bottleneck claims were removed by #469; they are **historical**, not current supported resource fields or computations. Fresh observations cannot reconstruct the removed historical accumulator. |
| Research methods and removed legacy KPI package | Research-local occupancy, waiting and flow oracles are not shipped definitions. The retired internal-scheduler KPI package is not a supported analytics substrate. |

The R-obs/R-hist distinction concerns **independent reconstruction from other facts in one fresh observation**, not whether Engine uses an internal accumulator. The result-affecting Engine interpretation, runtime observation/event contract, Determinism Contract and currently declared arithmetic remain unchanged by this ownership reconciliation. The current Engine supports no resource utilization, cumulative busy-time, occupancy or causal bottleneck measure. Supported captures can establish **different named methods** where their own proof obligations are met; they cannot retroactively recover uncaptured historical facts.

## Phase 2 — definition, evidence and method admission

Analytical meaning and physical custody are separate. The runtime controller owns command/advancement provenance and its attributed, trust-scoped closure; an evidence holder captures, qualifies and retains the required supported basis, changes and frontiers; the named-method owner defines sufficient inputs, refusal and calculation. A holder may serve multiple methods and a method may change definition custodian without moving a captured bundle. That does not make a shared definition stateless in all implementations, nor does it make promotion automatic: portability without semantic rework is conditional on carrying proof provenance from the outset.

For **any retained or promoted interval method**, the definition/result must remain attributable to its named meaning, `RunId`, `ModelFingerprint` (`ControlledRevisionId` only when authoritatively bound), interval/convention, basis `(S0,T0)`, relevant proof sequence/frontier and proof class. Controller attestor, excluded-input scope, command/fault/trust qualifications, exact producing Engine-definition provenance, and full ordered command/advance script (including budgets) are **claim-specific** obligations; a producing definition must never be invented from a fingerprint or build. Definition-specific proof must bind basis, observations, captured changes and any closure to the **same run**, reject a closure contradicted by held later changes before the claim's upper bound, and refuse a purported gap-free stream lacking a separately authoritative frontier. Producer change witnesses, supported observations and attributable controller closure are distinct proofs. Complete supported changes do not prove fault-free execution or that all commands were captured. These requirements are admission criteria for future named methods, not an invented universal production verifier; [runtime contract](../../architecture/runtime-contract.md#captured-execution-evidence-and-interval-determinacy) retains the shared producer/control/finality rules.

A Game-local named method is **permitted in principle**, subject to the separately concluded [Game diagnostic-evidence requirements](factory-design-game-diagnostic-evidence.md) and the consumer plan's method acceptance gate before product admission. Its research-local flow cases used a job-free basis, always-online resources, one product and constructive closed scripts; their results cannot be generalized without new evidence. A fresh current-state observation can support “no currently known work” or a known-work idle/starved label. An aggregate over a period needs finality; “no work left for the rest of the period” additionally needs a future-effective-input exclusion claim. No named utilization/flow definitions or playable Game implementation are admitted here. See the [consumer plan](../../planning/factory-design-game-consumer.md) and [blocked gate](../../planning/factory-design-game-vertical-slice.md).

Methods interpret supported outcomes, not a substitute Engine: they cannot infer hidden scheduler selection, unobserved historical step starts, authoritative causal bottlenecks, unissued commands, fault-free history, or deterministic replay from state-change streams alone. A controlled counterfactual uses actual Engine re-execution and complete explicit inputs.

## Reusable evidence, precise negatives and compatibility

The research established counterexamples worth retaining as future method-admission checks:

1. **Mixed-run basis (R1).** Combining a basis from one `RunId` with changes from another can falsely accept 10 occupied ticks when the same-run answer is 5; reject the bundle.
2. **Contradicted closure (R2).** A controller's stale closure bound before a held completion can falsely accept 100 ticks when five are justified; refuse it rather than trusting a clean call detached from held changes.
3. **Stale-holder proof frontier.** A driver that honestly exhausted through 100 cannot make a dispatch-only holder's 100-tick claim true; the post-advancement completion/frontier proves five ticks.
4. **Earlier valid prefix.** A later gap does not revoke a previously proven earlier half-open interval. Events exactly at the upper boundary are excluded; equal-time state transitions remain sequence-ordered.
5. **Snapshot/period/future horizon.** “No currently known work” from a fresh supported observation is not a proved idle-tail aggregate or a promise that no future input will arrive.
6. **Removed historical information.** A current observation with no `busyTicks` cannot reproduce its historical accumulator; supported captured events may answer a differently defined method but do not restore the removed field.

The research-only proof helper accepted R1 and R2 incorrectly. It is **not** a product verifier or a defect in the established runtime evidence contract. Other research-only fixtures/probes may be discarded after qualified conclusions and these reusable cases reach durable homes; their exact historical source files need not be retained as shipped tests.

Cross-revision claims are projection- and definition-specific: #465 removed inert scheduler markers without changing supported events for fully start-fixed inputs advanced only with time-bounded calls, while some positive-event-budget-stopped scripts **can** differ. #466 established the current captured-evidence/interval-finality contract. #469 removed the resource accumulator, so old results relying on that field cannot be recomputed from today's observation. These facts do not declare all past and present Engine results incomparable; compare only with the needed [exact-reference and equivalence evidence](simulation-analytics-cross-revision-comparability.md).

## Evidence and disposition

Phase 1 first classified current Engine- and Factory-owned facts and result arithmetic; the earlier conclusion about `busyTicks` is preserved only as historical evidence. Phase 2 compared retaining Engine-produced measurements, moving all derivations outside Engine, a staged method-specific approach, and a possible late-join execution-fixed aggregate. An early Phase 2 review returned `REOPEN` because it could not safely justify the proposed evidence-custody rule; an interim revision was not independently reviewed. A later complete report received genuinely independent **ACCEPT WITH QUALIFICATIONS** review. The six qualification families were transferred to the normative ownership rule, the Game consumer/gate, Governance producer-versus-holder provenance, this research state, and the still-candidate evidence/provenance method-refusal cases. The underlying workspaces are temporary research custody, not architecture, retained APIs, or a permanent report archive.
