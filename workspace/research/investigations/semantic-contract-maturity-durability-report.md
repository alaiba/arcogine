## Question

When should Arcogine promote a currently authoritative semantic contract into a durable immutable contract, what evidence must precede that promotion, and what compatibility/provenance obligations apply before versus after promotion?

The brief needs one sharpening: **durability is a scoped promise**, not a single guarantee of perpetual service. Definition preservation, historical decoding, execution support, outward compatibility, and physical retention are different obligations. The report evaluates which promise is justified, as well as when it begins.

## Decision at stake, scope, and non-goals

The immediate decision is whether first implementation/persistence should automatically commit Arcogine to permanent Factory policy support, and whether a named Engine design specification proves its complete interpretation mature. The spatial plan already holds work that would deepen those commitments. A result must explain how proving can continue without erasing historical facts or silently weakening existing promises.

In scope: the brief's Factory, Engine, runtime/transport, Governance, Operational, and changing-contract cases; evidence for promotion; consumers and custody; current-contract classification; and the smallest correction path. Out of scope: Factory composition, a replacement dispatch policy, analytics ownership, release dates, identifier implementation, migration implementation, or a general versioning framework.

## Executive conclusion

**Recommendation:** adopt **Candidate C: boundary-specific evidence-gated commitments with shared minimum rules**. Current normative authority should not automatically imply a perpetual compatibility obligation. Every attributable artifact and accepted historical occurrence must nevertheless remain non-rebinding from creation/acceptance, including during proving. Promote a specified contract revision and a specified support promise through an explicit reviewed decision after evidence covers the consumers, failure cases, evolution, and retention consequences of that promise.

**Do not retroactively call existing commitments provisional merely because no external product release was found.** Preserve `factory-model:v1`'s published meanings, golden vectors, and historical resolution; preserve the meaning already attributed to `engine-semantics:v1`. V2 has not shipped a fingerprint policy and is already an amendable normative design under its own §10. Engine v1 has implemented non-spatial conformance and a runtime identity, but lacks the spatial and propagation evidence needed to claim the entire specified capability released. The narrow intervention is to improve future promotion criteria and clarify those boundaries, not recycle v1 identifiers or discard controlled history.

The evidence rejects an automatic, universal freeze on introduction or first persistence. It also rejects Candidate B's absolute requirement for a later separate ceremony for every semantic obligation: non-rebinding of accepted history must apply immediately, and a mature externally constrained contract can be introduced with its support commitment when the evidence already exists. Introduction and support promotion must be distinguishable decisions; they need not always occur in different PRs or at different dates.

**Confidence:** high in the distinctions, non-rebinding rules, and rejection of automatic promotion; moderate in the proposed minimum gates and their sufficiency across future consumers. No external deployment/user inventory was available. That limits any claim that existing compatibility support can safely be removed. Independent review remains a completion condition of the brief before promotion, not a completed step of this run.

## Candidate models and proving cases

### Candidates

- **A — eager durability:** introduction/accepted specification or first publication/persistence triggers the durable promise. Strongest fair form: scope the initial contract narrowly, preserve exact historical meaning, and add a new identity only when meaning changes. This is simple, enforceable, and can be the right choice for a small published codec; it is not inherently incorrect.
- **B — mandatory separate maturity promotion:** normative but provisional first, then a later independent promotion after implementation/consumer/research evidence. Strongest form preserves immutable exact revisions during proving. Its distinctive claim is that introduction and promotion can never be one step.
- **C — boundary-specific commitments:** immediate non-rebinding/attribution obligations, plus an explicit support-promotion decision with evidence appropriate to each promise. Two useful descriptions are proving and durable-for-a-declared-scope; no mandatory intermediate state, age threshold, central registry, or implementation framework is required.
- **D — release-bound durability:** free evolution until a supported product release, then a freeze. A strengthened form preserves exact pre-release artifacts. Release is still its decisive trigger.

No Candidate E is needed. Prospective-only improvement while preserving existing commitments is a transition rule for C, not a different lifecycle.

### Concrete cases

1. **Factory identity and history.** A V1 design is recorded, changed, then restored to equal content in a third controlled revision. Later a spatial model becomes available. Old revision IDs must still resolve the original facts; equivalent content must not collapse historical occurrences; missing spatial facts cannot be defaulted into historical truth. Test golden-byte correctness separately from whether perpetual future decoder support is justified.
2. **Engine interpretation.** One current runtime reports v1 and reproduces the pinned recovery/ranking traces. Spatial transfer and version propagation are still incomplete. A proposed recovery fill changes outcomes; merely optimizing exact ranking does not. Can current behavior be normative and attributable without claiming an unimplemented whole contract mature or promising execution forever?
3. **Runtime versus transport.** A headless observation/event consumer works while legacy SSE still exports internal scheduler events. Adding Engine provenance and moving to post-authoritative events must converge the clients before a stable outward contract is promised. A working JSON serializer alone is insufficient.
4. **Governance and accepted identity.** Evidence correction follows a completed evaluation; a future Operational record corrects an earlier accepted assertion. ADR-0016 and ADR-0013 require new attributable records, but select neither a final Evidence format nor an Operational registry. Can those obligations bind before the implementation format stabilizes?
5. **Operational reliance before a release.** A pilot deployment uses a candidate model and an external adapter to authorize a consequential operation. Even if the product is called pre-release, the accepted command, authority, actual result and applied configuration must remain explainable. Contrast a disposable simulator benchmark with the same data shape used to justify that operation.
6. **A proving-stage equality correction.** Constructed, not an observed defect or selected Factory redesign: an importer and an editor discover that one `name` field conflates an operational label with a translated display label. They agree to split the fields; presentation-only relabeling should no longer alter the candidate policy's semantic identity. Old V1 explicitly hashes names and must retain that meaning. Compare a new provisional definition revision plus explicit regenerated fixtures with a permanently supported next policy and with an unqualified rewrite under the old name. Whether that split is actually right belongs to Factory composition/product evidence, not this report.

## Proposed minimum rule and promotion evidence

### Distinctions that must remain visible

| Concept | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Artifact immutability | A particular published model, capture, result or accepted record cannot silently change its identity-bearing content/provenance. A correctable statement is still an immutable record of what was stated. |
| Normative/current authority | The selected specification revision governs current supported work and interpretation. It can be superseded deliberately; it is neither an optional suggestion nor a promise of perpetual compatibility. Implementation disagreement remains a defect or explicit unsupported scope. |
| Semantic-contract durability | A declared obligation to preserve a named contract's historical meaning and the particular verification/resolution guarantees promised for it. A new contract can supersede it; the old name cannot acquire the new meaning. |
| Implementation release | A distributable implementation with declared capabilities. Build identity supports diagnosis/exact reproduction but is not, by itself, semantic identity. |
| External compatibility support | A promise about which clients/artifacts interoperate across specified versions and for what support/retirement scope. Its owner, adapters, migration and failure behavior must be explicit. |
| Controlled historical identity | Which authoritative occurrence was accepted. Repeated equal content can occur at different points in history. |
| Semantic content identity | Which authored content is equal under an identified equivalence/canonicalization policy. It is not approval, source authenticity, occurrence identity or universal comparability. |

The evolving object during proving is the **current contract line**, not an already identified artifact or definition revision. It can move to a new exact definition and end bounded support for an older experimental revision under its declared custody rules. Durability adds the promised continuing definition/verification/resolution obligations across future evolution. It is not a switch that makes same-identity rewriting acceptable before promotion. A provisional label also cannot excuse changing a live run's interpretation midway through execution.

**Proposed promotion event:** the owning authority accepts a decision naming the exact contract revision, the promise and supported scope, the evidence that justifies it, the affected consumers/artifacts, and evolution/retention/retirement obligations. Use the existing ADR/architecture/review process where the decision is hard to reverse; do not create another ledger or approval organization. A supported release may enact the decision but cannot substitute for it. Previously made explicit promises are accounted for as existing commitments, not reset by the new vocabulary.

### Evidence gates

1. **Meaning and scope:** settle the referent, equality, non-rebinding boundary, ordering/units/normalization, ownership, error behavior, and exact meaning of “supported.” Distinguish authored facts, interpretation, result identity, and representation. Unresolved questions that can change the proposed frozen scope block that scope; unrelated questions do not.
2. **Implemented path:** for an executable/serialized compatibility promise, exercise an end-to-end producer-to-consumer path over the whole promised scope, including rejected inputs and failure boundaries. A declaration or value object alone is insufficient. A purely logical invariant such as “accepted occurrence cannot be rebound” can be Accepted before code; that does not confer an implemented support claim.
3. **Consumer pressure:** demonstrate a concrete use and at least one independent challenge to the originating producer's assumptions. Independence means a distinct consumer task, implementation, or independently derived oracle/failure analysis; another copy of the same happy-path test does not qualify. Stronger requirements depend on the contract class below.
4. **Conformance:** pin independent expected outcomes and negative cases, not only round-trips through the same implementation. Include interaction cases, boundary arithmetic, ambiguous/missing provenance, and unsupported-version rejection. A byte grammar needs exact vectors; Engine meaning needs behavioral vectors; historical use needs reconstruction/correction/gap cases. Tests of wire bytes alone do not prove semantic meaning.
5. **Evolution and custody:** rehearse at least one realistic change, old/new coexistence or explicit refusal, migration/rebaseline classification, retirement, and missing-history behavior. Inventory actual accepted histories and promises. Specify who can retain/retire what and for how long; a hash or copied Git SHA is not a readability guarantee.
6. **Maturity assessment:** answer the bounded question: *For this exact revision and declared promise, what evidence shows the semantic/equality/ownership boundaries have survived implementation and relevant consumer pressure; what plausible remaining discovery would force a break, and why accept that risk now?* Compare freeze now, continue proving, and freeze a smaller scope. Use existing research/review artifacts where they answer it; do not force a second redundant report.
7. **Review and reconciliation:** high-risk identity/persistence/history promises require an independent adversarial pass on that assessment before durable promotion, then normal independent review of the reconciliation. Resolve material qualifications in the authority, not only in a report. This is the repository's existing research standard, applied to the freeze decision rather than a new process tier.

| Contract class | Minimum discriminating consumer/evolution evidence; independent consumer rule |
| --- | --- |
| Fingerprint/canonical artifact | One functioning publication and historical-consumption path, independently derived byte/digest vectors, equality sensitivity, strict decode/canonicality, and an old-artifact transition. If claiming cross-language interoperability, obtain an independent implementation or independently implemented conformance harness in another language; do not claim that handwritten grammar alone demonstrates it. A separately deployed customer is not mandatory, but an originating codec's self-round-trip is insufficient. |
| Engine semantics | Complete supported-scope behavioral characterization, deterministic rerun and interaction/failure cases, one actual consumer task, and an independently derived objective/counterexample review. A second Engine implementation is preferred corroboration where feasible, not a universal gate. Before claiming consumer neutrality across materially different tasks, exercise a second distinct task; two wrappers over one trace are not enough. |
| Runtime/transport | End-to-end domain-to-adapter-to-client use, schema/semantic projection tests, provenance completeness, unknown versions/types, upgrade and recovery behavior. A separately implemented client or a test consumer using only the published boundary is required for a public interchange promise. An external organization is not required. |
| Governance/history | At least one real acceptance-to-later-resolution/use path, plus correction/reuse/definition drift and unavailable-history tests. An independent reviewer must challenge historical meaning and producer ownership. One domain consumer can justify a narrow history promise; simulated external provenance cannot prove Operational integration. |
| Operational consequence | The actual accountable-use boundary needs demonstrated source/target correspondence, authority, failure/ambiguous-result handling and reconstructable accepted history before consequential reliance is admitted. Independent domain/safety scrutiny is required proportionate to the consequence. This is an admission constraint; production exposure must not be used to gather missing safety evidence. Multiple organizations are not a numerical requirement. |

These gates are recommended policy, not already enacted requirements or claims that this run executed the proposed demonstrations. A narrow already-proven invariant need not wait for a hypothetical second customer. Conversely, a large “whole platform” promise cannot use one originating consumer as evidence for untested tasks.

## Answers to the brief's sixteen questions

1. **Normative/current before durability:** one identified revision is authoritative for the declared present scope; implementations and consumers cannot pick incompatible meanings. Supersession changes what is current. It does not change what an older artifact meant. Current support can be bounded to an exact revision/environment with deliberate opt-in.
2. **Event that makes a contract durable:** explicit acceptance of a scoped preservation/support promise after the gates above. Reliance can expose an already owed obligation even if the team omitted a label. Neither an ADR's status nor `v1` spelling automatically defines all support guarantees.
3. **Separate introduction and promotion:** distinguish the decisions, usually sequence proving before support, but do not require different dates/PRs universally. An existing well-proven contract can be introduced durably. An accepted-history non-rebinding invariant must already hold at first acceptance.
4. **Minimum implementation evidence:** for concrete implementation support, one complete path and failure/transition evidence for every advertised scope component. V2 validation is not codec evidence; an Engine constant is not spatial conformance; an Evidence fixture is not a trusted source integration. Abstract architectural invariants may be accepted earlier without pretending otherwise.
5. **Consumer evidence and one originator:** an originating consumer can justify a narrow contract, but cannot alone demonstrate general cross-consumer meaning. Require concrete use plus independent pressure on its assumptions. Add a distinct consumer task whenever the promoted claim depends on such generality.
6. **Independent consumer:** required for a claimed public interchange boundary in the sense of independently implemented client/conformance use; a real second task is required for a cross-task semantic-neutrality claim. Preferred, not categorically required, for a single Engine or narrow historical store. Review independence is necessary for high risk regardless of consumer count; it is not a substitute for missing execution evidence.
7. **Fixtures:** preserve grammar vectors for content identity, behavior vectors for interpretation, round-trip plus rejection for decoding, and acceptance/correction/reconstruction for history. Inputs/expected values need a specification or independently derived oracle. Freeze facts the contract owns, not incidental DTO layout, timestamps or random run IDs. Proving fixtures may change through attributed revisions; durable fixtures must keep historical meaning.
8. **Required maturity research:** the exact-scope question in gate 6, with surviving alternatives and realistic break cases. This investigation supplies the repository-wide decision method, not a positive maturity verdict for every future contract. A focused section using existing reviewed evidence may answer a narrow promotion; a major unresolved identity/ownership boundary needs its own bounded investigation and independent pass.
9. **Provisional persisted artifacts:** allow explicitly disposable fixtures to be regenerated or retired; invalidate them for further use when interpretation is unsupported; migrate by creating a new attributable artifact with source revision, transformation identity, supplied facts and declared losses; retain relied-on originals/definitions for their promised custody. Invalidation concerns admissibility, not whether an old event happened. Do not overwrite accepted bindings or call a changed experiment the same result.
10. **Exact history without eternal development support:** retain immutable definition/input/result snapshots sufficient for the promised explanation during a declared custody horizon. An exact manifest can reference retained content; an unresolved label or build name cannot replace it. Keep old definitions/readers only as the actual contract requires; unsupported execution fails explicitly. Deliberate retirement may bound availability only where permitted, with gaps disclosed. Existing V1 permanent resolution is a stronger promise and is not canceled by this proposed general rule.
11. **ADR, maturity, release and support:** ADR status records an architectural decision; maturity is an evidence judgement about a specified promise; release is an implementation event; external support is a client/artifact obligation. Record them distinctly near the owning contract. Accepted logical invariants can coexist with unimplemented representation and unsupported transport. No central state machine follows.
12. **Pre-durable naming:** future proving contracts should carry an unmistakable provisional marker and an exact immutable definition revision, for example a policy-version token shaped like `provisional-<definition-revision>` or an equivalent explicit descriptor. That is illustrative syntax, not a selected encoding. Every semantic change gets a new exact revision even during proving. Do not use an unqualified `v1`, `latest`, branch tip, or build alone as proof of permanent support or exact semantics. Promotion records the relationship to the tested revision and must not silently rehash/alias identities. Existing named v1 meanings remain intact.
13. **After durability:** never reuse the identifier for changed equality, semantic membership, canonicalization or result-affecting interpretation; preserve the immutable definition and required fixtures; keep promised historical resolution; preserve accepted occurrence lineage and provenance; fail explicitly for unsupported meanings. Retirement of execution is allowed for Engine under ADR-0015, not permission to erase attribution. Cross-version comparability remains explicit and consumer-owned, not inferred solely from equal/different version strings.
14. **Premature current promotions:** the universal diagnosis does not survive. V1 has a precise implemented narrow codec/history promise and actual dependent code; insufficient evidence establishes that withdrawing it is better. Its permanent future-support trigger would not satisfy the proposed prospective maturity assessment merely by having shipped. V2 is normative but not yet released/frozen under its §10; calling it already permanently frozen is inaccurate. Engine v1's implemented attribution is defensible; treating its entire spatial specification as mature from the constant/non-spatial tests would be premature. ADR-0016/0013 acceptance establishes logical constraints, not unimplemented format support.
15. **Truthful correction/rebaseline:** prefer the prospective route in the next section. Preserve historical V1 names and bindings, clarify pre-release V2 and incomplete Engine scope, and use distinct provisional revisions for genuinely changed future proving contracts after reconciliation. A stronger retroactive withdrawal of promised decoder support is not authorized by this evidence. If pursued separately, it requires owner knowledge of consumers/retained histories, an explicit superseding support decision, and a migration/retention path; no new policy may reinterpret old fingerprints or control revisions.
16. **Falsification:** reopen for an actual incompatible external or retained-data constituency; inability to preserve meaningful history with bounded provisional custody; repeated producer/client divergence despite exact revision pinning; evidence that mature support can be inferred reliably from the proposed simpler trigger; or lifecycle effort with no changed support/design outcome. Contract-specific missing implementation or consumers also block a positive maturity verdict without falsifying the general distinction.

## Surviving invariants and what did not survive

1. An exact historical referent never silently acquires new content or meaning, whether a contract is proving or durable.
2. Current authority, historical non-rebinding, release, and future support are distinguishable responsibilities. State the promise rather than infer it from a word such as “accepted” or “version.”
3. Durability must preserve the scope actually promised: Engine attribution is not eternal execution; Factory permanent historical resolution is stronger than a retained identifier.
4. A change in authored content, a change in interpretation, and a new controlled occurrence are different changes. Migration must preserve those distinctions.
5. Real reliance/consequence can require preservation before product release. Unimplemented or planned consumers cannot establish that reliance or prove support maturity.
6. Existing promises constrain transitions. A prospective policy cannot make history disappear.

Failed universal claims worth retaining as negative knowledge: first publication proves maturity; a formal release proves maturity; provisional permits same-identity reinterpretation; every Accepted ADR freezes a concrete API; every named version requires perpetual execution; and absence of external release records proves absence of users. Also rejected: a mandatory later promotion ceremony for an already-proven narrow invariant.
