## Question and decision at stake

What enduring rules, if any, should govern semantic evolution across Arcogine, and which propositions in PR #360 are instead repository process, current-contract decisions, or implementation work?

The decision is whether to ratify PR #360's universal proving/promoted mechanism and section-level freeze rule, or restructure it around a smaller common constraint with domain-owned commitments. The investigation does not choose Factory composition, new Engine behavior, storage/codec architecture, release syntax, or Operational authorization/retirement mechanisms.

## Executive conclusion

**Recommendation: make an explicit ground-zero transition, withdraw the pre-reset support estate through a new Accepted reconciliation, and establish a smaller post-reset identity-and-obligation model. Substantially rewrite PR #360 before ratification.**

The repository owner has now made a decisive input: everything presently in the repository is the complete pre-reset estate; no client, retained artifact, external store, release, or deployment relies on it; and no pre-reset support commitment needs to continue. That input authorizes a prospective reset decision. It changes the earlier recommendation to preserve Factory V1 and Engine V1 promises: those commitments remain historical facts in the repository record but need not remain promises of the post-reset system.

The brief's proposed reduction is still incomplete. An exact semantic or historical binding must not be rebound within the supported estate. Continuing availability, decoding, execution, migration, and interoperability are distinguishable commitments. **After the reset, they are constrained by the accepted uses and published promises they serve; they are not freely optional merely because no promotion form was written.** A digest whose referent has vanished can remain non-rebinding while failing every useful historical-explanation promise.

The smallest defensible common rule is:

> Preserve the meaning and provenance of exact historical bindings. Evolve definitions and interpretations through distinguishable changes. State and satisfy the support and retained-basis obligations required by the uses Arcogine accepts; changing or retiring support must neither rewrite history nor silently discharge those obligations.

This is an architectural consequence of Charter continuity, provenance, explicit reality, and consequence-sensitive controls. No new product thesis or Charter amendment is established by this evidence. The rule does not impose perpetual storage or executability, a universal version scheme, or a platform lifecycle type.

The four-layer hypothesis survives as a **separation of authority and change rate**, not four independent ontologies or four mandatory documents. Product principles already exist. A compact enduring rule belongs in the Architecture Overview; an ADR records its significant decision and any supersession; development policy can describe the evidence process; owning domain specifications hold concrete support promises and current adjudications; planning owns executable work.

Confidence is **high** that an explicit reset can remove the inherited compatibility estate without erasing repository history, and high in the distinctions needed afterward. Confidence is **moderate** in the proposed minimum review mechanism. The evidence does not prove that a universal two-state lifecycle is necessary, nor that publication-triggered support is always wrong. It remains a legitimate local choice when deliberately scoped and justified.

## Owner-directed ground-zero transition

### Decisive owner input

The repository owner states that no party needs or cares about any pre-reset behavior, that no legacy client, retained artifact, store, release, deployment, or external consumer exists, and that the repository is the complete estate. This is an owner decision and operational fact supplied on 2026-09-19, rather than an absence inference from source searches or public GitHub metadata.

The decision being recommended is deliberately narrow:

- Pre-reset compatibility, decoding, execution, migration, retention, and interoperability promises may be withdrawn.
- Existing identifiers, artifacts, fixtures, APIs, codecs, and historical-resolution paths do not define the supported post-reset estate merely because they remain in Git history.
- Git history is retained as delivery history. Existing Accepted ADRs remain historical decisions until a new Accepted reconciliation supersedes them; the reset does not authorize rewriting or deleting their meaning.
- The reset does not discard Charter direction, architecture ownership, determinism, provenance, explicit reality, or consequence-sensitive control where those are independently valuable to the new product.

### First post-reset estate

The reconciliation should establish one explicit reset boundary and state that only contracts introduced after that boundary can create supported obligations. It should not claim that every old value has been migrated, decoded, or made invalid in every possible private copy; it states that Arcogine no longer offers or owes support for the pre-reset estate.

For each future contract that Arcogine publishes as supported, retains as an authoritative artifact, exposes as an external compatibility surface, or relies on for consequential use, its owning authority must define the exact referent, supported scope, necessary retained basis, failure behavior, and retirement/change path. A future support obligation may arise at introduction where the owner deliberately makes that promise. No universal `proving`/`promoted` state, central registry, or historic migration framework is required.

The reset is not a license to reuse an identifier within the new estate for changed meaning. Use a fresh namespace, an unmistakable reset-qualified identity, or another clear boundary selected during reconciliation; the report does not choose the syntax or require implementation before the architecture decision.

## Minimal enduring principles proposed for ratification

These are recommendations, not edits to authority.

1. **Truthful binding.** An exact reference preserves its identity-defining referent, interpretation and intrinsic provenance. Corrections, changed interpretations and later occurrences are distinguishable; mutable aliases and accumulating identities must declare what changes and what remains fixed. Non-rebinding is not a claim that the original statement was true.
2. **Accountability follows accepted use.** Before accepting a use that claims historical explanation, replay, interoperability or consequential accountability, establish the basis and support that claim requires. Preserve it for the applicable obligation; disclose gaps and refuse unsupported claims. A maturity label, release date, or missing declaration cannot waive existing obligations.
3. **Meaning, availability and implementation differ.** Historical meaning, retained evidence, decoding, execution and external compatibility have different scopes and dependencies. No perpetual executor or storage estate follows from naming alone. Conversely, preserving a digest alone does not fulfil a promised explanation. Representation may evolve only without silently changing semantic bindings or breaking separately owed compatibility.
4. **Evolution accounts for commitments in scope.** State the successor's meaning, supported scope, compatibility evidence and retirement effects. Preserve original accepted bindings and domain ownership. Narrowing an in-scope promise requires an explicit authorized transition. The owner-directed reset is such a transition for the complete pre-reset estate; it does not rewrite that estate's historical record.

These constrain all tested domains without imposing common identity equality, one retention duration, one maturity state, or one implementation. Principles 1 and 3 are largely explicit already; principle 2 exposes the qualification missing from the brief's reduction; principle 4 makes their interaction during change reviewable. This is a synthesis of existing authorities with a proposed cross-domain application, not a claim that main already contains exactly this general rule.

### Why historical truth and support are separable, but not independent

Non-rebinding can hold even when an artifact becomes unavailable. That is a property of what the identifier denotes. A supported historical explanation additionally needs the facts and definitions it claims to explain; its retention dependency is real. A permanent decoder promise implies maintaining some executable decoding capability, but not the original binary or permanent simulation execution. Scope the dependency rather than pretending the dimensions never interact.

An accepted Operational continuation is a decisive counterexample to “all historical support is optional”. ADR-0013 requires sufficient accepted-history and divergence evidence for continuity. Recording a new custody expiry cannot bypass that requirement. The current authority permits particular bounded compaction and explicitly distinguishes other loss/fork outcomes; the generic doctrine must preserve those distinctions.

## Subordinate mechanism: the smallest useful proposal

Use **a compact support declaration in the owning contract**, backed by the normal decision/review process. Record exact semantic revision or identity, supported use/input scope, obligation source (existing promise or newly admitted use), preserved basis and custody, decoding/execution/interface guarantees, evidence and limitations, and change/retirement behavior. Do not require all categories to have implemented machinery when only a logical invariant is being accepted.
