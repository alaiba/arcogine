## Result and promotion boundary

The report proves useful facts about today's supported evidence. It does not prove that its
prospective boundary is the only surviving ownership model. In particular, it leaves a viable
alternative substantially unevaluated: retain current results; allow explicitly specified Engine
measurements when a concrete requirement justifies them; keep consumer interpretations outside;
establish common measurement ownership only for an identified shared semantic requirement.
This is more than a packaging variant: it changes the prohibition in R2, the present ownership
claim, and whether evidence/provenance research is ready to promote.

There is also a specific false premise in the Model 1 rejection: Engine semantics §10.1's
zero-denominator conventions apply to named existing results. They do not require every future
Engine measurement to encode insufficient evidence as zero, and do not prohibit separately named
results with explicit refusal. Coupling is a genuine architectural cost, not a contradiction.

These are reasons for **REOPEN**, under researching.md §9's viable-omitted-model and falsified
load-bearing-claim criteria, rather than simply attaching editorial qualifications to the same
promotion decision. This does not select Engine-rich growth, require moving any existing field,
or reject future shared analytics. A revised report can still recommend the mixed boundary, but
must discriminate it from the surviving alternative and correctly state what is preference,
what is demonstrated, and what remains conditional.

**Durable reconciliation of the report's complete R1–R5/promotion package may not proceed on this
review.** Current-state observations remain usable evidence. A revision intended for promotion
needs its own exact report coordinate and applicable independent review. The workspace remains
in active custody; the umbrella stays unresolved and downstream questions are not promoted here.

decision reads them; fresh observations close the supported current view without mandatory full

2. Execution primitives and a deliberately changed evidence/support contract, with measurement

3. Keep current results, admit separately named measurement contracts when demonstrated requirements
   need them, and defer a common owner until a shared requirement is identified.
4. Establish a shared measurement responsibility now, without implying a module or API.

arithmetic and saturation; unfinished concurrent work and offline capacity; shared waiting without a
unique resource; zero-time boundaries, no-op internal markers and missing events; caller-supplied
denominators; same challenge policy with a different producing basis; additive named measurements
versus redefinition; controlled changes versus claimed mechanisms; actual versus presumed support
commitments during development.

These reconstruct the central distinction: information sufficiency constrains an evidence contract;
it does not alone choose its semantic owner. The report's own R1–R5 vocabulary was not used to
construct this list. No synthesis-seed material was consulted.

## Material challenges and results

### A and C — Candidate completeness and Engine-rich growth

**Challenged:** report Executive conclusion items 1–3, candidate table, PC-2, and the claim that
all variants either collapse or change no decision.

**Evidence:** Engine semantics §1/§10.1–§10.2; current development/support rules; D1; occupancy
definitions; the game and Governance ownership restrictions.

**Result:** a standardized Engine measurement with consumer-owned presentation/diagnosis remains
viable. It need not claim to exhaust diagnosis, make occupancy equal bottleneck identity, or
reuse throughput's zero-window sentinel. A new named result can define its own admitted inputs and
refusal without changing any old metric. Its Engine ownership and definition-change cost must be
explicit; nothing in these proving cases makes that cost unacceptable for every concrete use.

The report fairly acknowledges in its self-challenge that coupling is a cost. Its candidate verdict
and R2 nevertheless upgrade that cost into a universal admission ban. The claim that earlier
results become non-attributable is also too strong: §1 says they are not attributable to today's
definition **merely from** model fingerprint/run ID/status. An unchanged result boundary may have
separately established compatibility; a new measurement does not itself falsify earlier execution.
That proof has a cost, but is not forbidden.

**Effect:** the prospective winner has not been established against its strongest competitor.
Re-evaluate Engine-owned standardized measurement with separate diagnostic interpretation and the
staged-admission candidate below. If the mixed boundary still wins, identify the concrete
independent-evolution/support requirement and accept the tradeoff explicitly. Do not present a
discretionary architectural preference as an executable falsification.

### B — What strict facts-only actually loses

**Challenged:** experiments B/C/C′ imply that facts-only fails as a semantic model.

**Evidence:** full experiment, rerun, observation record shapes, runtime fresh-view/retention rules.

**Result:** removing busyTicks and completedSalesValue while preserving everything else about
today's fresh observation cannot retain the same information. This narrow falsification is sound.
It does not cover a consumer with retained accepted commands/events, additional primitive
projections, or separately captured analytical state. In B prices differ; in C availability
commands differ. Those are legitimate distinguishing inputs when the candidate expressly owns
their capture. Rerunning Engine under such inputs is not proof that the original snapshot alone
contained them, nor permission for analytics to simulate dispatch.

An outside analytical accumulator can serve its own fresh analytical view without making Engine
state event sourced. A replacement primitive projection can trade size and complexity for outside
calculation. These require explicit support/evidence changes, and are not free refactors, but need
not recreate precisely the same Engine-owned sum. The report itself identifies per-order price
plus completion order as an alternative. That is additional primitive evidence, not the sum itself.

**Effect:** retain B/C as strong reasons against an unqualified drop-in removal now. Narrow the
verdict to unchanged-input/unchanged-support Model 2. Information loss is not an ownership theorem;
whether the extra contract is worth adopting remains a cost/benefit decision.

### D and J — Shared owner now, and downstream admission

**Challenged:** three existing references presuppose a current shared analytics owner, so
evidence/completeness/provenance becomes promotable on acceptance/reconciliation.

**Evidence:** Factory Design §10.2; Governance evidence §11–§13 and actual fixture-only integration;
game plan's explicitly unresolved ownership; diagnostic-evidence candidates; Charter §6; the
evidence/provenance brief's two-part trigger.

**Result:** the references establish a legitimate *place for* a common calculation owner and a
constraint against accidental game ownership. They do not select a named shared measurement or
show why one concrete requirement needs the same definition across uses. Governance's producer
rule is conditional on a producer's contract and can consume Engine, domain-local or later shared
results. The game restriction deliberately awaits this investigation and cannot decide its answer.
Factory's example utilization threshold is a shared concern, but still lacks an identified
measurement use/boundary sufficient to choose among its several legitimate meanings.

The report's own unresolved-unknowns section leaves the first concrete admitted analytics use open.
Occupancy demonstrates an input-completeness dependency; it does not alone prove a shared semantic
requirement. Research-substrate reuse is explicitly not production ownership.

**Viable omitted alternative:** retain current Engine results; keep current generic-game-analytics
restrictions while unresolved; permit explicit consumer-specific methods within their own scope;
admit common measurement definitions when an identified shared use or a concrete core verification
requirement needs them. Standardized functions outside Engine need no standalone capability or
module. Select their semantic owner with that requirement. This is not the claim that measurements
must always stay local, the binary alternative used in the report's decision-at-stake section.

Requiring two *shipped* consumers would itself be unjustified. Charter §6 permits a single concrete
core-capability requirement to drive foundations. What is missing is the bounded requirement and
observable acceptance evidence, not a deployment count or a particular package topology.

**Effect:** this alternative changes the present ownership and downstream decision, so it cannot
be dismissed as placement. Only the truthfulness-dependency half of the downstream trigger is
demonstrated. Keep evidence/provenance conditional until a concrete reusable responsibility/use is
identified, or revise the report to establish that use. Do not close Governance's ownership gap or
claim the game gate will be satisfied simply by adopting a category name.

### E — R1(c), R2 and attribution

**Challenged:** R1(c) admits counts/sums with no attribution beyond the run; busyTicks is a
per-resource attributed sum; R2 classifies attribution/population choices as measurement policy.

**Evidence:** completion handler and supported JobStepCompleted payload; accumulator specification;
waiting/occupancy derivations.

**Result:** execution-authoritative attribution to the machine that performed a step is different
from a method choosing which resource caused a delay. R1(c)'s literal wording fails to make that
distinction. Completed counts also select completed entities; counts/sums are not policy-free
merely because of their arithmetic form. Current Engine ownership is clear from declared semantics.

**Effect/qualification:** repair the rule to distinguish aggregation along authoritative identities
and transitions from method-selected population, interval or causal attribution. Do not imply every
possible run-to-date count/sum must become an Engine result. This is a repairable rule ambiguity,
not a reason to move busyTicks or reject the anti-second-engine principle.

### F — Retained lead time and throughput

**Challenged:** R1(d) arbitrarily exempts two policy-laden fields; Model 3-strict would be cleaner.

**Evidence:** exact current result definitions, A, conformance fixtures, and development support rules.

**Result:** retaining useful declared results is reasonable; no proving case requires immediate
removal. A prospective rule can coexist with an explicit finite legacy exception. Recomputability
enables migration but does not justify it. However, current development definitions are correctable
and no retained compatibility promise follows merely from existing tests. The report's statement
that no analytics owner exists to receive the definitions is circular if it means a semantic role;
if it means no implemented service/API exists, that distinction must be stated.

**Effect/qualification:** preserve these fields and exact sentinels unless an explicit later change
is chosen; record retention as a revisitable cost/benefit choice, not necessity. A future named
measurement may differ without silently reinterpreting these results. The caller-parameterized
throughput helper also remains under existing Engine arithmetic and must not disappear from the
exception/support accounting simply because it is not a RuntimeObservation field.

### G — Measurement without a second engine; comparison authority

**Challenged:** permitted waiting, occupancy and qualification secretly require Engine decisions.

**Evidence:** WaitingWorkByStepOracle, ProcessingIntervals, DispatchProfileOracle, their tests,
supported payloads, Challenge comparator, Governance applicability rules.

**Result:** current non-spatial waiting is grouped by operation step from jobs/model; shared
eligibility is preserved instead of inventing a unique physical queue. Dispatch/completion events
give actual assignments and intervals, so occupancy need not predict ranking or admission.
Wait-duration measurement uses actual readiness/completion and dispatch times, not a simulation.
Transfer execution is absent, so bound-destination waiting and transfer decomposition remain rechecks.

Measurement-basis compatibility can be computed without Engine internals when required inputs are
actually supplied; missing exact Engine basis must remain missing. Challenge currently checks
challenge and evaluation-policy identity. Its opaque model/run references are not proof of Engine
definition equality or a generic simulation comparability contract.

**Effect/qualification:** R3 survives for supported outcome measurement. A common measurement
definition may own its comparability prerequisites, but must not take Governance's use-specific
applicability/reliance or Challenge's scoring compatibility away from their owners. Same-revision
comparison still needs complete result-affecting inputs and a stated claim; shared numeric deltas
do not automatically own every consumer's qualification policy or causal interpretation.
